決算発表サマリーの続報として、まずLegalBrain エージェントについて掘り下げます。
昨年5月23日のLegalBrainエージェント提供開始から17.3カ月でARR10億円に達し、国内上場企業の主要SaaSプロダクトの中で最速でのARR10億円達成となりました。

6月29日に日本初の統合型法務AIエージェントへ刷新してからは、伸びが一段と速まりました。利用はまず弁護士を中心に広がり、いまでは企業の法務部門にも及んでいます。11月2日には長島・大野・常松法律事務所への提供が始まり、所属弁護士からパラリーガルなどのスタッフまで、事務所全体で使われる予定です。

ふだん使っている生成AIの画面からも、LegalBrain エージェントを使えるようにしました。ClaudeやMicrosoft Copilotと連携したことで、利用者は日本の法令や判例、法律書籍に基づく調査結果を出典付きで得られます。使い慣れた画面に組み込むことで、利用の場面と使う人の層をさらに広げていきます。

これほど急速に広がった背景には、2つの理由があります。
第一に、日本の弁護士不足です。人口あたりの弁護士の数はアメリカの約10分の1にとどまり、その60%超が首都圏に集中しているため、地方では弁護士の人手不足が深刻です。
第二に、AIの活用に積極的な20代から50代が、国内の弁護士の中心を占めていることです。2004年に司法制度改革で法科大学院が開校して弁護士が増え、いまでは77%超を20代から50代が占めます。高齢化が進む日本にあって、弁護士の業界にはAIの活用に積極的な若い世代が多くいます。
この人手不足と、AIに前向きな担い手の多さが、急速な売上の伸びにつながっています。加えて、弁護士ドットコムはデータを自らのグループ内に持っています。全裁判官・全検察官が使う判例データベース「判例秘書」と、出版社から預かった法律書籍のデータをAIが直接参照するため、回答には根拠となる文献が出典として付き、精度を自社で磨き続けられます。

汎用AIは確率で答えを予測する仕組みである以上、存在しない判例を引用する誤りを根絶できません。だからこそ、調査や草案はAIに委ね、最終的な判断は弁護士が自ら確かめて下します。この分担が定着すれば、AIは弁護士を不要にするのではなく、弁護士がより生産的に働けるようにする存在になると考えています。

政策の面でも追い風が吹いています。7月21日に閣議決定された官民投資ロードマップでは、バーティカルAI（領域特化型AI）が注力分野に掲げられ、その中に弁護士業務でのAI活用の促進が明記されました。8月21日には法務省が、ビジネス分野のAI法務支援サービスと弁護士法72条の関係について考え方を公表し、利用者が安心して使える環境が整いました。

弁護士ドットコムのビジョン「まだないやり方で、世界を前へ。」を、リーガルAIの領域で形にしていきます。LegalBrain エージェントのこれからを、どうか楽しみにしていてください。

🚨 Another major scandal is coming out of NVIDIA.

And this one has nothing to do with Jensen Huang's affairs.

According to a Wall Street Journal report, Nvidia was offering billions in financial guarantees to AI companies that could not afford its chips, so they could keep buying them.

Some of these deals were being pushed by Jensen's own son-in-law, Nico Caprez, who became a vice president in January. His team was telling small cloud companies and data center builders that Nvidia could arrange credit support for them within a couple of weeks.

The goal was simple: turn them into long term buyers of Nvidia GPUs.

And the size of Nvidia's commitments has exploded. Its own filing shows $530 BILLION in commitments and guarantees, up from $184 billion just one quarter earlier.

Inside the company, CFO Colette Kress was raising concerns. She kept asking how these guarantees were priced and what Nvidia was really getting back.

In an August meeting, Jensen cut her off and called her "a deer in the headlights."

She was also the one who cut Nvidia's guarantee on OpenAI's Ohio data center from $250 billion to $105 billion.

Now Jensen is gone, and the CFO who questioned these deals is running Nvidia.

To understand why this is a big deal, you need to understand the AI funding loop.

Here is how it works:

1. Nvidia puts money into an AI company, or guarantees its loans and leases.
2. That company uses the money and credit to build a data center.
3. The data center is filled with Nvidia GPUs.
4. Nvidia reports those GPU sales as revenue.
5. If the company cannot rent out all its computing power, Nvidia agrees to buy the unused part.

SO NVIDIA IS THE INVESTOR, THE LENDER, THE SUPPLIER AND THE BACKUP CUSTOMER, ALL IN THE SAME DEAL.

The money goes out of Nvidia and comes back to Nvidia as sales.

CoreWeave is the clearest example. Nvidia owns a stake in it, sells it chips, and agreed to buy up to $6.3 billion of its unused computing capacity through 2032.

OpenAI is the biggest. Nvidia is guaranteeing up to $105 billion for its Ohio data center, supplying all the chips inside it, and invested $1.5 billion in the company building it.

Nvidia's own filing for the quarter ending July 26 shows where the money is tied up:

• Supply commitments for memory and chip wafers: $279 BILLION, up from $119 billion.
• Guarantee on the OpenAI Ohio data center: up to $105 BILLION.
• Deals to buy unused cloud capacity from neoclouds: $36 BILLION.

Nvidia's actual liabilities on its balance sheet: $91 BILLION.

That means the commitments outside the balance sheet are almost 6 times bigger than the debts on it.

Nvidia does not lose this money unless something breaks. A guarantee only costs Nvidia if a customer cannot pay.

But this is where it gets uncomfortable.

The WSJ says Jensen himself had started to panic. He warned executives that the biggest cloud companies were moving to their own chips to depend less on Nvidia.

At the same time, the wafers and memory Nvidia had committed to buy were coming at him like an "avalanche."

So the biggest customers were trying to leave, Nvidia was locked into buying huge amounts of supply, and smaller customers were being kept alive with Nvidia's credit.

And things were already starting to crack:

• Nvidia paused some of its financing deals in August.
• Nearly half of the $80 billion in data center bonds issued this year were rated junk.
• Neocloud stocks crashed this week, with Nebius down over 20% and CoreWeave down 17% at the lows.
• OpenAI raised less than it wanted, at a lower valuation.

And now Kress is the one deciding whether this continues.

If she pulls back on the financing, the companies that depended on Nvidia's money will have to borrow elsewhere, in a debt market that is already nervous.

If they cannot, they stop buying chips.

And if they stop buying chips, Nvidia's revenue drops.

That is the risk of a loop. It works perfectly when money keeps flowing, and it hurts on the way down.
